What it is

Plane Sound tracks and logs aircraft overhead and warns you when one will interfere with your sound recording.

How it works

The app tracks aircraft using live position signals and knows how loud they are, using published aircraft noise data and the physics of how sound travels. Weather conditions like temperature, humidity and wind are taken into account so Plane Sound can be as accurate as possible.

The result is knowing how long before the next aircraft will affect your recordings and how long until the aircraft is clear.

What it's built on

Plane Sound combines EASA aircraft-noise data (the certified noise figures used in aviation) with ISO 9613-1, the international standard for how air absorbs sound over distance. It draws on live aircraft tracking for real-time positions, speed and altitude, along with live weather at your location.

Privacy

Plane Sound runs on your device. There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no advertising or behavioural tracking.

Location

Your location is used only to find nearby aircraft and show activity around you. It is requested only when you choose current location, enter coordinates, or search. It is never stored anywhere but on your device.

On your device

Your settings, chosen location, microphones and contamination log are saved locally in your browser so the app remembers them between sessions. None of it leaves your device except as below.

Network

To work, the app sends your coordinates to its aircraft-tracking and map-lookup services to get results back. That is the only data sent.

Microphone

If you turn on live sound input, audio is analysed in real time on your device only. Nothing is recorded, saved, or sent anywhere.

Plane Sound does not sell user data.
